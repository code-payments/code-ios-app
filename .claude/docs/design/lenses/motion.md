# Motion lens

Judges whether a change's motion is feedback the user needed, at the house's pace, in the house's forms, and whether it survives Reduce Motion, interruption, and a 120 Hz phone. The reviewer reads the diff for the patterns below, names the rule each finding breaks and the path:line that already does it right, and files anything about feel under "Needs a device" instead of guessing from code.

## Rules

1. **Animate only what answers a question: where did this come from, did my tap register, what just changed.** Motion that answers nothing is a tax paid on every repetition, so cut it before tuning it. `MentionSuggestionList.swift:83-86` turns animation off with `.animation(nil, value:)` where it misled.
2. **Let frequency set the elaboration: a 100-times-a-day action gets press feedback and nothing more, an occasional one gets one spring under 0.3 s, a first-time moment may run long.** A spring that delights once annoys on the hundredth repeat. `NotificationPermissionScreen.swift:60` runs 0.8 s at bounce 0.4 after a 0.3 s delay because it plays once per install; `ButtonPress.swift:24-28` is the whole budget for a button.
3. **Never animate a change driven by typing: a filtered list, a caret, a character count, a mention candidate swap.** The user is already ahead of any curve, so animation reads as the UI lagging their input. `MentionSuggestionList.swift:86`.
4. **Write every spring as `duration:` plus `bounce:`, duration 0.17 to 0.45 s, bounce 0 to 0.35.** `.spring(duration:bounce:)`, `UIView.animate(springDuration:bounce:)` and `ChatSpring` share one physics (`dampingRatio = 1 - bounce`, `ChatMotion.swift:40`), so a number tuned in one form ports to the others untouched; `.spring(response:dampingFraction:)` and the `Animations.swift` presets are legacy and take no new entries. `MarketCapSlider.swift:119`, `ToastController.swift:141` (form only; its timing is an open decision).
5. **Choose the curve by the job: a spring for anything that settles into place or that a finger can interrupt, `.easeOut` or `.smooth` for a fade, `.easeInOut` only for a view already on screen moving between two resting states, `.linear` only for elapsed time, `.easeIn` never on an entrance.** A spring retargets from live velocity when interrupted and a fixed curve restarts; an ease-in holds the movement back at the moment the user is watching for a response. `YouScreen.swift:616` picks the spring by the gesture's outcome; `WalletScreen.swift:322` fades the deck on `.easeOut`.
6. **Give bounce 0 to any value another view tracks every frame: an inset following a bar, a height a sibling measures, a scroll offset following chrome.** Overshoot in the leader drags the follower past its resting place and back. `ChatMotion.swift:117` (`keyboardScroll`), `:128` (`replySurface`), `ConversationBottomBar.swift:23-28`.
7. **Move with the keyboard on the keyboard's own duration and curve, never a house spring.** Any other curve fights the system's and the bar visibly detaches from the keyboard's edge. `ChatScreenViewController.swift:891-895` builds the options from the notification's curve; `ChatViewController.swift:364-384` pins the transcript inside UIKit's own keyboard block.
8. **Run exits 20 to 30 percent quicker than their entrance, with bounce 0 and less travel.** The user has already decided to leave, and an overshoot on the way out reads as the thing coming back. `ChatMotion.swift:154-157` (`reaction` 0.32 s in, `reactionExit` 0.2 s out, no bounce).
9. **Enter from scale 0.9 to 0.95 plus opacity, anchored at the edge the view comes from; a glyph, pill or toast no taller than 44 pt may start at 0.4 to 0.6; a bar or toast that belongs to a screen edge may slide from it with `.move(edge:)` instead; nothing starts at 0.** A real object has a shape before it arrives, and a scale from 0 hides the opacity ramp behind a speck. `ChatMotion.swift:167-175` and `:238-256` grow a row from the sender's bottom corner; `ConversationBottomBar.swift:672-674` says why the send arrow starts at 0.6.
10. **Give every conditionally mounted view a `.transition` that mirrors its entrance, driven by one `.animation(_, value:)` on the view that owns the condition, and never add a `withAnimation` at the dismiss site on top of it.** A view with no transition blinks out and severs the context its entrance built; a second transaction gives the exit a curve the entry never had. `ConversationBottomBar.swift:202-204`.
11. **Views that move as one share one spring: a surface and its scrim, a card and the page that follows it, a row and the pills inside it.** Two timings on one movement read as two unrelated events. `WalletScreen.swift:325-331` (the page follows on the card's own curve, delayed, committed in the same frame); `ChatMotion.swift:158-160` (`reactionReflow = reflow`).
12. **Scope `.animation(_, value:)` to the view that changes and the value that changes it; never `.animation(.default, value:)` on a container, never `.animation(_)` without `value:`.** An unscoped animation animates every layout that happens to change in the same frame, including text wrapping and rows the user never touched. `ConversationBottomBar.swift:163-166` places the modifier inside the row's padding so the row's own position is left alone.
13. **Sequence on completion, not on a timer: `withAnimation(_:completionCriteria: .removed)` with its `completion:` in SwiftUI, the `completion:` block in UIKit; a delay is only for a designed hold, and a chain is at most three steps.** A spring is still settling when its nominal duration is up, so a timer hands off early and the two halves judder. `WalletScreen.swift:300-316`; `ChatMotion.swift:191-198` is a three-part hold with a stated reason for each part.
14. **Pass `options: .allowUserInteraction` to any UIKit spring that moves a view the user may tap mid-flight.** UIKit drops touches on animating views until they settle even though `hitTest` still returns them. `ReactionPillRowView.swift:351-356`; `common-pitfalls.md:7`.
15. **Under Reduce Motion keep the opacity and color change and drop the movement and scale; never return early with nothing.** The setting asks for less motion, not less feedback, and a hard cut is the jolt it exists to prevent. `ReactionPillRowView.swift:250` (scale 1, alpha still fades); `ReactionPillView.swift:129-137` (a push becomes a fade). SwiftUI reads `@Environment(\.accessibilityReduceMotion)`, UIKit reads `UIAccessibility.isReduceMotionEnabled`.
16. **Press feedback is one press everywhere: a shrink to 0.95 (`Metrics.buttonPressScale`) on `Metrics.buttonPressAnimation` (spring 0.2 s, bounce 0), a dim to 0.7 instead under Reduce Motion, through `ButtonPress`.** Every titled house button does this, so a button with its own press reads as a different control. Exceptions: tiles, rows and cards keep their own press; icon-only circles keep a dim; picker segments take none, since the sliding selection is the feedback; the public group keeps its touch-down haptic. (Revised 2026-11-19: replaces "an opacity drop, never a scale".) `ButtonPress.swift:24-28`; `PressButtonStyle.swift`; `TrayCloseButton.swift:69`.
17. **One haptic per event, fired on the event, never from an animation's completion.** A haptic that lands when the spring settles arrives late and lies about when the thing happened. `ChatSwipeToReply.swift:178-183` fires at the threshold crossing, not the settle; `HomeTabView.swift:165-169` fires after the gate so a hold that opens nothing stays silent.
18. **Roll numbers with `.contentTransition(.numericText())`, swap symbols with `.contentTransition(.symbolEffect(.replace))`, and replace a string inside a sized capsule by keying identity with `.id(text)`.** Interpolating a label's width draws the new glyphs against the old frame, so the text spills or slides into place. `BalanceHeaderButton.swift:32`; `ConversationBottomBar.swift:667`; `ChatMuteStatusLabel.swift:73-81`.
19. **Put `.matchedGeometryEffect` before `.frame`, and never `.transition(.identity)` on a matched view's parent.** Wrong order fails silently as two views fading at their own positions; `.identity` removes the parent before the morph can interpolate. `TokenCardStack.swift:212`; `common-pitfalls.md:15`.
20. **Nothing animates on first appearance: a first load, a cell scrolling in with pills it already had, an indicator already at rest.** Nothing the user did not cause should move when a screen appears. `ReactionPillRowView.swift:271-273` (`animatesChanges` is false until the row has drawn once); `ChatMotionTests.swift:182-185`.
21. **Scroll-driven motion tracks the finger: a fade or offset tied to the scroll position has no spring of its own, a programmatic `setContentOffset(animated:)` or `scrollTo` lands on the system curve or one house spring, and nothing animates a value the scroll view already drives.** A second animation on a scrolling value fights the gesture and reads as lag. `TipConversationsScreen.swift:497-545` (the chip row's pull) is the one scroll-driven surface today; judge it on the device.

## Flipcash specifics

- **The model is `ChatMotion`** (`FlipcashUI/Sources/FlipcashUI/Chat/ChatMotion.swift`): one `ChatSpring(duration:bounce:)` vends `.animation` for SwiftUI (`:56`), `.animate {}` for `UIView` (`:62`), and `.layerAnimation(keyPath:)` for a `CAShapeLayer` path (`:82`), run for `settlingDuration` so the travel is never cut short (`:92`). Sixteen named springs, each with a `///` stating its intent; a spring changes there, never at a call site, and its comment stays true.
- **The house range is that file's range:** 0.17 s (`sendButton`) to 0.45 s (`corner`, the slowest on purpose so a bubble run regrouping reads as settling), bounce 0 to 0.35 (`reaction`). `replyMerge` at 0.5 s sits outside it for the reason in its own comment (`:129-131`) and is not a precedent.
- **Forms outside chat.** Screens write `.spring(duration:bounce:)` and `.smooth(duration:)` (`ConversationBottomBar.swift:93`, `WalletScreen.swift:308`, `ToastController.swift:141`, `EmojiPickerSheet.swift:253`). `.spring(response:dampingFraction:)` survives at 17 sites in `Flipcash/Core/Screens` (`HomeTabBar.swift:56`, `BillOverlayView.swift:141-142`, `YouScreen.swift:75-79`, `ColorEditorControl.swift`) and `usingSpringWithDamping:` at `MessageLiftOverlay.swift:80,229`. Convert one only when its file is already open for motion, as `duration = response`, `bounce = 1 - dampingFraction`, same feel, and say so in the PR.
- **Toasts are not a precedent yet.** `ToastController.swift:141` runs the entrance and the exit on one 0.4 s, bounce 0.2 spring with a y-scale swell matched to Android (`FloatingToast.swift:147-172`); rule 2 budgets an occasional action under 0.3 s and rule 8 a quicker exit, so the toast's timing is an open decision in `design-decisions.md`.
- **`Animations.swift` is frozen** (`FlipcashUI/Sources/FlipcashUI/Transitions/Animations.swift`): `easeOutFastest`, `springFaster`, `springFastestDamped` and `crossFade` serve `AmountField`, `SwipeControl`, `ToastContainer` and `StockChart`, nothing new. A shared value for a new area gets its own `enum` with `///` intents on the `ChatMotion` pattern.
- **A transcript batch update inherits the enclosing spring:** `ChatViewController.swift:448-452` wraps `reload(using:)` in `spring.animate`, `:571-578` picks `insertion` when rows come or go and `reflow` when they only resize, and `:1140-1170` supplies the starting state. That is the only way ChatLayout's insertion gets a spring; no per-cell animation goes on top.
- **Tests pin the physics:** `FlipcashTests/Chat/ChatMotionTests.swift:28-39` holds one row per spring (`dampingRatio = 1 - bounce`, `stiffness = (2π / duration)²`, `damping = 4π × dampingRatio / duration`). A changed spring updates its row, a new spring adds one, and the PR says the table moved on purpose. Run `./Scripts/test.sh FlipcashTests/ChatSpringPhysicsTests`. `replySurface`, `replyMerge`, `reaction`, `reactionExit` and `reactionChange` have no row yet.
- **Haptics.** `FlipcashUI/Sources/FlipcashUI/Haptics/Haptics.swift` is the intensity scale: `softest` 0.1, `soft` 0.35, `medium` 0.6, `hard` 0.8, `hardest` and `buttonTap` 0.9, `tap` 1.0, `vibrate` the error notification. SwiftUI views use `.sensoryFeedback(_:trigger:)` with a guard closure: `.impact(weight: .light)` for an arrival (`ConversationScreen.swift:822-829`), `.selection` for a highlight moving (`ChatListChips.swift:81`), `.impact(weight: .heavy)` for a commit (`SwipeControl.swift:74`). A UIKit gesture keeps one generator and prepares it ahead of the threshold (`ChatSwipeToReply.swift:54,154`).
- **Press feedback is the 0.95 shrink** (rule 16). A label that draws its own capsule takes `.pressable`; a custom `ButtonStyle` calls `.buttonPress(isPressed:)`. The keypad's key pop (`KeyPadView.swift:422`, scale 1.5, no animation) is a keyboard key, not a button, and stays.
- **Reduce Motion** is handled in UIKit (`ReactionPillRowView.swift:268`, `ReactionPillView.swift:131`, `ChatTypingIndicatorCell.swift:138`, `LinkCardShimmerView.swift:130`) and, in SwiftUI, by the shared button press (`ButtonPress.swift`); few SwiftUI screens read `accessibilityReduceMotion` themselves, so a new SwiftUI entrance with movement is where a review looks first.
- **Sandbox:** `--motion-sandbox` (and `--motion-sandbox-group`), DEBUG only, replaces the app with `ChatMotionSandboxViewController` (`Flipcash/Core/FlipcashApp.swift:65-83`, `FlipcashUI/Sources/FlipcashUI/Chat/ChatMotionSandbox.swift`): the shipping `ChatViewController` on a scripted send, Delivered, Read, typing, reply, reset. Before/after recordings of chat motion come from it, never from a live send.
- **Judged on a 120 Hz iPhone.** The simulator is for quick looks and Slow Animations; a verdict on feel from a simulator recording is not a verdict.

## Flag on sight

- `.spring(response:` or `.spring(dampingFraction:` in added lines (4).
- A new `static var` in `Animations.swift` (4).
- `withAnimation {` with no curve (5); any `withAnimation` around a `TextField` binding, search results or mention candidates (3).
- `.easeIn(` on an entrance (5).
- `bounce:` above 0.35 (4); any `bounce:` above 0 on a value whose change resizes a bar, an inset or a measured height (6).
- `duration:` above 0.45 outside onboarding (2, 4).
- `.transition(.scale)` with no `scale:`, `.scale(scale: 0)`, `.scaleEffect(0)`, `.scaleEffect(x ? 1 : 0)` (9).
- `if show { View() }` with no `.transition` (10); `.transition(.identity)` near a `matchedGeometryEffect` (19).
- `.animation(.default, value:` on a `ZStack`, `VStack` or screen root; `.animation(.easeOutFastest)` or any `.animation(_)` with no `value:` (12).
- A `.delay(` chain longer than three steps; `Task.sleep` or `asyncAfter` sized to a spring's `duration` standing in for its completion (13).
- `UIView.animate(withDuration:` on a gesture-driven or interruptible view; `usingSpringWithDamping:` in added lines (4, 5).
- `ChatSpring.animate {` or `UIView.animate` moving a tappable view with no `.allowUserInteraction` (14).
- `guard !UIAccessibility.isReduceMotionEnabled else { return }` or `if reduceMotion { return }` (15).
- `.scaleEffect(configuration.isPressed` or `.opacity(configuration.isPressed` inside a titled button's `ButtonStyle` instead of `.buttonPress(isPressed:)`; `.buttonStyle(.plain)` on a titled button (16).
- `Haptics.` or `impactOccurred()` inside a `completion:` block or after a `withAnimation`; `UIImpactFeedbackGenerator(` created inline in a SwiftUI view (17).
- `.onAppear { withAnimation` outside onboarding (20).
- `.matchedGeometryEffect` after `.frame` (19).

## Before / After / Why

| Before | After | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `withAnimation(.spring(response: 0.35, dampingFraction: 0.8)) { isOpen = true }` | `withAnimation(.spring(duration: 0.35, bounce: 0.2)) { isOpen = true }` | Same physics, house form (4): the number now reads against `ChatMotion` and ports to `UIView.animate(springDuration:bounce:)` unchanged. |
| `if showsPanel { Panel().transition(.scale) }` under `.animation(.easeIn(duration: 0.5), value: showsPanel)` | `if showsPanel { Panel().transition(.scale(scale: 0.95).combined(with: .opacity)) }` under `.animation(.spring(duration: 0.3, bounce: 0.15), value: showsPanel)` | A shape before it arrives (9), a spring that reverses cleanly when closed mid-open (5, 10), 0.3 s instead of 0.5 (2). |
| `guard !reduceMotion else { return }` ahead of `withAnimation(.smooth(duration: 0.25)) { shown = true }` on `.offset(y: shown ? 0 : 12).opacity(shown ? 1 : 0)` | `.offset(y: shown || reduceMotion ? 0 : 12).opacity(shown ? 1 : 0)` with the same `withAnimation` | Gentler, not zero (15): the fade still tells the user the view arrived, only the travel is gone. |

## Needs a device

- **Feel of a bounce or a settle.** Only a 120 Hz iPhone shows it; the simulator runs on the Mac's clock and GPU, so a bounce that reads right there is unproven. Judge the branch installed on the phone (`device-run.sh` in the design skill's `scripts/`), not a simulator recording.
- **First-frame jumps, snapped exits, pairs drifting apart.** Simulator, Debug, Slow Animations (Cmd+T), then the same surface at 1x on the phone; a jump at the start or end of any enter or exit is a finding (8, 10, 11).
- **Chat motion before/after.** Launch with `--motion-sandbox` (`device-run.sh -- --motion-sandbox`, or `xcrun simctl launch` with the argument), record both sides of the change from the same script; what differs is the change.
- **Reduce Motion.** Settings, Accessibility, Motion, Reduce Motion on; walk every changed surface; a hard cut or a lost fade is a finding (15).
- **Interruption.** Open and close each surface as fast as possible and reverse a drag mid-flight; a jump to the end state or a restart from the beginning is a finding (5, 10).
- **Taps mid-flight.** Tap a pill, row or button while it is still moving; a dropped touch is a finding (14).
- **Keyboard.** Raise and dismiss it with the transcript at and away from the bottom; the bar rides the keyboard's edge with no gap and no overshoot (6, 7).
- **Scroll-driven surfaces.** A slow drag, a short pull and a fling on each: the fade tracks the finger, and a programmatic scroll lands once without a second animation (21).
